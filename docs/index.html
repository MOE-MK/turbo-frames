T35 Structure

Built for durability.

The bottom plate is one continuous piece of 5 mm T700 carbon, with no separate arms to crack or loosen. Every edge is a curve. The lit lines are all 159 curves in the plate, and none of its corners is sharp.

Why a unibody works here ↓

T35 Electronics

Stack it your way.

Designed around the DJI O4 Pro, and it works with almost anything. Mount a 25×25 AIO, a 20×20 stack or a 30×30 stack, with room for other video systems like the DJI O3.

Every mounting pattern on the bottom plate, lit.

T35 Power

9×9 or 12×12 motors.

Slotted motor mounts take both bolt patterns. The frame is designed around 16xx–18xx motors at 3000–4000 KV, on a 4S 850–1000 mAh LiPo.

The blue rings mark the 9 mm and 12 mm bolt circles each slot covers.

T35 Radio

Antenna slot, built in.

A slot at the back of the top plate locks a whip antenna in place, so you don't need a 3D-printed mount for it. Tested and proven.

T35 Assembled

No props in view.

The props stay out of the camera's view for a clean, uninterrupted feed. Ten M2 × 25 mm standoffs hold the plates together. M2 is on purpose, so the standoffs can absorb hits.

Durability · Turbo35

Built for the concrete jungle.

The Turbo35 is made for bandos, concrete and hard hits. Durability isn't a feature added at the end: the frame was designed around it, from the thickness of the carbon to the shape of every corner. It can be built under 250 g, but that's not what it's for.

A unibody, on purpose

On a 5-inch frame, a unibody can be a drawback. At 3.5 inches it's the point. One continuous 5 mm plate means no arm joints, no arm bolts to back out, and no separate arms to snap at the root.

Rounded everywhere

Sharp corners are where cracks start. Every edge and cutout on the Turbo35 flows into a curve, so there are no built-in weak points.

Checked in simulation

The design was run through simulation to find its weaker spots before the first plate was cut.

Tested by the community

Built, flown and crashed by pilots in the FPV community.

M2 standoffs, by design

M2 standoffs are chosen to absorb hits, so they take the energy instead of the carbon.
